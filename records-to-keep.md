# Records an owner-operator should keep

The IRS says to keep records for 3 years in the usual case, and employment tax
records for at least 4 years
([IRS: How long should I keep records?](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-long-should-i-keep-records)).
Many accountants tell truckers to keep everything longer, and truck purchase
papers for as long as you own the truck.

| Record | How often | Why you need it |
|---|---|---|
| Settlement statements | Every week | They prove revenue and most deductions in one document |
| Fuel receipts and fuel card statements | Every fill, every month | Date, state and gallons build your IFTA return with your ELD miles. Your base jurisdiction sets how long it wants them kept |
| Truck and trailer papers | When bought, then yearly | Purchase agreements, loan statements and the lender's year-end interest figure. Depreciation and the loan balance both come from them |
| Logs or ELD reports | Every trip | Days away from home support the per diem deduction, and miles by state support IFTA and IRP |
| Bank and card statements | Every month, every account | They are what your books are proven against |

## Folder layout that works

```
2026/
  2026-10-09/   settlement.pdf, fuel receipts, anything paid outside the settlement
  2026-10-16/
  statements/   bank, business card and fuel card statements by month
  truck/        purchase agreement, loan statements, year-end interest letter
```

One place per week, paper or phone. That folder is what your bookkeeper, your
accountant or an auditor will ask for.
